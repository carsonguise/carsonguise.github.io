Understanding Ethel Cain’s Cultural Impact
Ethel Cain (Hayden Silas Anhedönia) is an artist who has been able to make her impact
loud and clear. Her unique perspective with her heavy songwriting and production has captivated
many, with roughly four million monthly listeners on Spotify. Her hits range from the alternative
rock anthem “Crush” to the gut-wrenching “Strangers.” Hayden has been making music under
different aliases, such as Atlas, White Silas, and the most well-known being Ethel Cain. Under
Ethel Cain, Hayden has been able to build up Ethel’s character and create a whole storyline that
expands upon her own personal life. Through this, she has been able to cultivate a die-hard
fanbase named the "Daughters of Cain" that have a love for her southern-gothic style, but also
for the fact that her songs cover many topics related heavily to gender, identity, self-exploration,
and intense emotional storytelling. Her album “Preachers Daughter” is a perfect example of how
pivotal her storytelling is. This album explores many topics, such as her own identity as a
transgender woman, her relationship to religion, and her relationships in general. This album is
essential in understanding her impact in terms of gender, identity, and expression.
To further one’s understanding of Ethel’s impact, it is important to investigate her
discography deeply. The most important piece of work and one of her most acclaimed is
“Preachers Daughter,” which was released on May 12, 2022. Many of the songs in this album
talk about heavy topics that can each be talked about in depth. The third and final single off this
album was “American Teenager,” talking about the idolization of the American Dream and how
things aren’t always like that. In this song there is one lyric that says, “The neighbor's brother
came home in a box, but he wanted to go, so maybe it was his fault another red heart taken by
the American dream” (Cain, 2022). Hayden is directly talking about the darkside of extreme
patriotism and the cost it has on families. Likewise, it talks about how Ethel never fit into high

school because of the idealistic view society has of it. She knows she will never be like the
cheerleaders and have a normal experience, something many of her fans really connect to her
with. After this song, the storyline devolves into Ethel reminiscing about her lover Willoughby
Tucker and how they fell out of love. She then runs away and gets picked up off the side of the
road by her new lover Isaiah Abram, who she begins to trust and fall in love with but then he
ends up torturing her and cannibalizing her. The song “Ptolomaea” represents this time in the
storyline, highlighting the extreme betrayal of trust she faced in a dark and haunting song. She is
hallucinating off the drugs that Isaiah gave her, resulting in the heavy melodic theme of the song.
This song is more than just to add to add to Ethel’s story line but was written by Hayden after
dealing with being raped by a man as a teenager (Ethel Cain Wiki, n.d.). To finish off the
summary of Preachers Daughter, one of the last tracks on the album is “Sun Bleached Flies.” At
this point in the story Ethel is dead and she is regretting stepping away from her faith and
recounting her life. The main lyric that sticks out in this section is “God loves you, but not
enough to save you” (Cain, 2022). This line has stuck with many fans who have a similar
experience growing up in tough religious experiences. This ability to portray heavy events in her
life is why the Daughters of Cain are a cult like following because Hayden is so vulnerable about
her experiences. This album is not her only piece of work that portrays her impact when it comes
to identify, but her other works like “Willoughby Tucker, I’ll Will Always Love You,” “Perverts,”
and “Inbred” have had renewed abilities to foster communities of fans.
There is something to say about the way Ethel Cain has been able to build a community
of cult-like fans since most of her music can be considered to not fit the norms of the music
industry. Her songs talk about topics that can be sensitive to listeners as well as melodically
challenging to listen to. Her album “Perverts” is a good example of this, being an album filled

with drone music that even some of her biggest fans could not get through listening to. A study
done touched on the fact that a lot of people are drawn into music like this through morbid
curiosity (Powell et al., 2025). This phenomenon is the fascination of death and related
phenomena, topics that Cain covers in various avenues. The aesthetic she chooses to follow can
often be described as grim, giving fans another reason to be attracted to her work due to this idea
of morbid curiosity. To further this topic, many of Ethel Cain’s fans identity as a part of the
LGBTQ+ community, something that Hayden herself is a part of and has helped her to further
the community she has built. This is even more important when it comes to gender and identity
apart of Ethel Cain’s fanbase. Research from a survey conducted on LGBTQ+ identifying people
concluded that participants talked about how music was a way of connecting to queer
communities (Jones et al., 2025). There is a very intersection of music and identity, especially
when it comes to the LGBTQ+. Thinking about global pop stars like Lady Gaga, Taylor Swift,
Ariana Grande, a lot of their diehard fans are the LGBTQ+, more specifically gay men. This is
because of the communities these artists have curated for their fans. Lady Gaga is a great
example of this, being an advocate for self-expression and finding yourself. She is avid about
vouching for gay rights and having a safe space for the LGBTQ+, leading to her massive support
from the LGBTQ+. Ethel Cain has done this in a similar fashion, just not to the pop extremes
Gaga and Grande have done. In creating this cult-like following comes with the benefits that die-
hard fans do most of their marketing for them. In these scenarios, superfans spread the word
about the artist they love through channels like word of mouth, interacting with their content on
social media, posting content relating to the artist, and much more. Ethel Cain has profited from
this environment with her cult following. Many viral moments have been created with Cain
interacting with her fans via livestream and other avenues. Moments like these further emphasize

how the creation of communities with like identities in music can help both the artist and their
fans. Likewise, Cain partners with pro LGBTQ+ groups like the Trevor Project or the Ally
Coalition to support her fans. This has allowed her to foster a following that can feel safe to
attend events she hosts or to relate to her music even more.
There is more to be said when it comes to Ethel Cain’s rise to fame. As mentioned
previously, Hayden has been making music under different aliases for years, yet Ethel is the one
to garner her the most success. This can be linked to the recent trend of slower songs rising to
fame. As noted in 2018, there was a downward trend in happiness and brightness in songs and
their success but an upward trend of sadness in songs (Interiano et al., 2018). This can be seen in
the following years with the rise of artists like Olivia Rodrigo, Rex Orange County, Cigarettes
After Sex, and other artists who create sadder music. Most of Ethel Cain’s music can be
categorized as sad, meaning this trend in sadder songs being more popular benefited Cain. While
this remains true, it also remains true that Hayden has had to face a lot of barriers in the music
industry for being transgender. There are many barriers that have held back trans music artists in
the music industry. The industry tends to favor artists they deem “commercially safe,” and safe to
them means cisgender artists. This pushes down many trans artists and causes them to have
delayed careers. Many trans artists have suffered from these systematic barriers, such as Sophie,
Arca, Kim Petras, Cortisa Star, and many more trans music artists. This raises the discussion
about cis-passing trans people being “liked” by society more because they don’t look like they
are trans. Some artists who are trans don’t talk about themselves being trans because of the
negative connotation society has placed on trans people, as well as the fact that it is getting
increasingly dangerous to be trans. This can be seen all around the world with legislation being
passed to directly affect trans people, especially in places like the United States. By the same

songs that are shorter, coming to around three minutes for the whole entire song. Ethel Cain’s
music often doesn’t follow this standard, having songs lasting three minutes to fifteen minutes.
Most of the songs off her most recent album are longer, with the last song “Waco, Texas” being
fifteen minutes and fifteen seconds long. Ethel Cain has seen a lot of progression throughout her
career, starting from an indie artist to filling her sets at massive festivals like Coachella. The
community and identity she has created for this community has carried her very far in her career
and continues to do so.
Ethel Cain’s story and impact matter because it points in the direction that there needs to
be more diversity within the music industry. This stems further than just the artists we listen to
and targets the employees of record companies, streaming services, management companies, and
more. As seen in all businesses, diversity and cultural evolution calls forth innovation, creativity,
and inclusivity. Music is heavily related to culture and has the power to change culture (Mehr et
al., 2019). While having a diverse pool of artists people can listen to is great, the staff of the
7
companies that run these artists needs to be just as diverse. The impact that Ethel Cain has had on
the music industry and the way she has been able to create a community for her fans is
something that we need more of. Having more diversity can allow people to have someone they
can listen to and connect with on a deeper level and allow subcultures to thrive even more.
Ethel Cain’s impact goes deeper than the music she creates. Her rise to fame has allowed
her to reshape the way identity, gender, and storytelling are in music. The communities she has
created have a culture people can connect with deeply. She has challenged preexisting notions on
marketability within the music industry and has opened doors for artists through the troubles she
has faced as a trans music artist. She has shown that being authentic can have better results than
being a robot to what the industry thinks is profitable. Overall, Ethel Cain has had a career path
that calls forth for a more diverse music ecosystem: from the artists we see on stage to the people
who work for them and the people who work in the industry.
